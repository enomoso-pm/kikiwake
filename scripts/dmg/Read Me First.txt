Getting started with Kikiwake

Kikiwake processes audio and text only on this Mac. It uses the internet only to download the AI models the first time
and to check for a new version at launch (it only asks for the latest version number). No audio or text is sent.

The app is available in Japanese and English. It starts in your Mac's language, and you can switch at the top right of the window.
Transcription currently supports Japanese recordings only.


1. Drag "Kikiwake" onto the "アプリケーション" (Applications) shortcut.

2. The first time you open Kikiwake, macOS asks you to confirm it.
   Kikiwake isn't registered with Apple's paid developer program, so at first macOS says it can't be opened.

   macOS 15 or later:
     1) Open "Kikiwake" in Applications. When a warning says it can't be opened, click "Done"
        (don't click "Move to Trash").
     2) Choose Apple menu > System Settings > Privacy & Security.
     3) Under "Security" near the bottom, you'll see a message saying that "Kikiwake" was blocked.
        Click "Open Anyway" next to it and enter your password.
     4) When asked again, click "Open Anyway". Kikiwake opens.

   macOS 14:
     In Finder, Control-click "Kikiwake", choose "Open", then click "Open" again.

   After the first time, it opens normally. After you replace it with a new version, you need to confirm it again.

3. The first time, Kikiwake downloads the AI models (about 3.2 GB). Then just drag in a recording and name the speakers.

4. When you use recordings in Documents, Desktop, Downloads, an external drive, or Google Drive, macOS may ask
   whether Kikiwake can access files in that folder. Click "Allow" (Kikiwake reads the recording and saves the
   transcript next to it).
